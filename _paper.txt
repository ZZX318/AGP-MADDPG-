 a b s t r a c t
Cooperative trajectory planning for multi unmanned aerial vehicle (multi-UAV) systems in three-dimensional 
(3D) environments is hindered by sparse rewards, long-horizon decision making, and unstable inter-agent coor-
dination. To address these issues, an A* Guidance Point based Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient 
(AGP-MADDPG) framework is developed, which integrates planning-based prior guidance with attention-based 
value estimation. In this framework, an improved A* planner with multi-objective constraints generates global 
reference paths by jointly considering path length, safety margin, and inter-UAV separation. Subsequently, dy-
namic guidance points are constructed along the reference path according to the UAVs’ positions, converting 
sparse feedback into dense guidance rewards to enhance exploration eﬃciency and training stability. To miti-
gate input dimensionality explosion and irrelevant interaction noise in the centralized critic, we incorporate a 
multi-head self-attention module. This module adaptively aggregates cross-agent information, improving both ac-
curacy and scalability of value estimation. Simulations in basic and extended scenarios show that AGP-MADDPG 
consistently outperforms baselines in convergence speed, mission success rate, decision eﬃciency, and trajectory 
quality, demonstrating its eﬀectiveness and robustness in cooperative multi-UAV trajectory planning.
1.  Introduction
Multi-Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (multi-UAV) trajectory planning and 
cooperative control play a pivotal role in various missions such as air-
ground coordination, emergency rescue, warehouse logistics, and large-
scale monitoring tasks (Cao et al., 2024). The core challenge lies in
simultaneously achieving safe obstacle avoidance and eﬃcient goal nav-
igation within a three-dimensional (3D) space constrained by obstacles 
and multi-agent interactions. Traditional path planning methods, in-
cluding the A* algorithm, Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT), and 
Artiﬁcial Potential Fields (APF), typically possess clear geometric inter-
pretability and global reachability (Debnath et al., 2024). Nevertheless, 
these methods often struggle with high-dimensional dynamics and envi-
ronmental uncertainties, and their dependence on complex mathemati-
cal modeling may limit real-time performance in cluttered and dynamic 
multi-UAV scenarios (Darlan et al., 2025).
Conversely, Westheider et al. (2023) pointed out that deep reinforce-
ment learning (DRL) can achieve strong adaptability in continuous con-
trol and dynamic environments, making it a popular choice for UAV 
navigation and trajectory planning. However, as noted by Kong et al. 
(2024), end-to-end reinforcement learning often suﬀers from ineﬃcient 
exploration, unstable convergence, and safety violations in long-horizon

tasks with sparse rewards and delayed credit assignment. This challenge 
is particularly pronounced in cluttered 3D multi-UAV trajectory plan-

creasing task diﬃculty from simple to complex or introducing sub-goal
sequences, thereby enhancing the success rate and controllability of 
complex navigation tasks.

In multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), Centralized Train-
ing with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) has become a widely adopted 
paradigm because it combines the training advantage of centralized 
value estimation with the deployment ﬂexibility of decentralized poli-
cies (Lowe et al., 2017). In this framework, each agent acts using only 
local observations, while the centralized critic leverages cross-agent in-
formation during training to facilitate value estimation and credit as-
signment. As a result, the performance of CTDE heavily depends on 
the critic design. Prior studies have shown that centralized critics are 
closely related to the scalability and stability of cooperative MARL, and 
that suitable optimization strategies can further improve convergence 
behavior (Lyu et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2022). Nevertheless, the conven-
tional MADDPG critic typically adopts direct concatenation of all agents’ 
observations and actions, which is not well suited to cooperative multi-
UAV trajectory planning. Such a design causes the critic input dimension 
to grow rapidly with the number of agents, increasing the diﬃculty of 
value approximation and reducing sample eﬃciency. Meanwhile, it fails 
to explicitly diﬀerentiate the importance of inter-agent interactions, al-
lowing redundant or irrelevant information to interfere with value es-
timation. This limitation is especially pronounced in long-horizon tasks 
with sparse rewards, where the critic must jointly model obstacle avoid-
ance, target reaching, and inter-agent coordination, often leading to lim-
ited stability and scalability.
Based on the above analysis, we propose an A* Guidance Point 
based Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient framework (AGP-
MADDPG) for cooperative multi-UAV trajectory planning in 3D simu-
lated environments. As illustrated in Fig. 1, each agent receives local 
observations 𝑜𝑖 from the environment and executes a decentralized actor 
policy with Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) noise for continuous exploration. 
During training, AGP-MADDPG shapes dense guidance rewards based on 
A* guidance points and employs a multi-head self-attention centralized 
critic to adaptively aggregate cross-agent information for stable value 
estimation. The main contributions of our work are summarized as fol-
lows:
• We design an improved A* based reference path generation module 
for AGP-MADDPG, which incorporates multi-objective constraints to 
provide structured and safe planning priors for cooperative multi-
UAV learning.
• We construct a dynamic guidance point mechanism based on the 
reference path, which transforms sparse terminal rewards into dense 
guidance signals to improve exploration eﬃciency and convergence 
stability in long-horizon tasks.
• We develop a multi-head self-attention centralized critic to selec-
tively aggregate cross-agent information, reducing redundant inter-
action noise and improving value-estimation stability and coopera-
tive performance within the evaluated settings.
2.  Related work
2.1.  Planning prior guided trajectory planning
In long-horizon trajectory planning, traditional algorithms oﬀer 
global reachability but struggle with high-dimensional dynamics, 
whereas end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) often suﬀers from 
sparse rewards and unstable convergence. To address these limitations, 
hybrid approaches have become a prevailing paradigm, leveraging plan-
ning to provide global topological priors while utilizing RL for local con-
tinuous control (Yuan et al., 2023).
Hierarchical frameworks typically decompose the tasks into manage-
able sub-problems using graph structures or waypoints. Francis et al. 
(2020) proposed Probabilistic Roadmap based Reinforcement Learning 
(PRM-RL), combining Probabilistic Roadmap with learned local policies 
to enhance long-distance navigability. To mitigate exploration ineﬃ-
ciency, Kästner et al. (2021) transformed global guidance into traceable 
sub-goal sequences, while Wang et al. (2024b) injected heuristic search 
constraints directly into the policy search space to improve sample ef-
ﬁciency. In multi-agent domains, such fusion is often integrated with 
explicit safety constraints. For instance, Feng et al. (2025) introduced 
a hierarchical framework integrating experience graphs and safe poli-
cies to achieve scalable, collision-free navigation. In short, prior hybrid 
methods mainly use planning outputs as discrete sub-goals for hierarchi-
cal execution, whereas our approach uses the planned path as a prior 
reference path to construct dynamic guidance points and dense reward 
shaping for long-horizon multi-UAV learning.
Focusing on control execution, Zhou et al. (2024) demonstrated that 
modularizing navigation and collision avoidance behaviors enhances 
both convergence stability and interpretability. For local trajectory plan-
ning, Feiyu et al. (2024) utilized Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic

2.2.  Centralized critic and attention mechanisms
While the CTDE paradigm is widely adopted in MARL, directly con-
catenating the joint observations and actions in the critic often in-
troduces interaction noise irrelevant to value estimation. Therefore,
attention mechanisms have been widely used to adaptively weight and 
aggregate inter-agent interactions (Iqbal & Sha, 2019). In dynamic en-
vironments, attention-based methods have been proven eﬀective in en-
hancing collaborative collision avoidance and information aggregation. 
Guan et al. (2022) integrated attention with communication to improve 
actor-critic learning in crowded scenarios, while Lin and Ma (2023) 
leveraged heuristic trajectory information as attention-based guidance 
signals. To address scalability, Wang et al. (2023) utilized improved 
attention modules to enable eﬀective planning under limited ﬁelds of 
view. Similarly, Zhao et al. (2024) leveraged the Graph Attention Net-
work (GAT) to model neighbor contributions and multi-agent coupling 
without explicitly expanding input dimensions.
Furthermore, self-attention mechanisms have gradually become ef-
fective tools for handling multi-agent interactions in MARL. Wen et al. 
(2022) proposed the Multi-Agent Transformer (MAT), which frames 
cooperative MARL as a sequence modeling problem, leveraging an 
encoder–decoder attention mechanism for scalable interaction model-
ing. Building on this, Wang et al. (2024a) proposed the Role-based 
Multi-Agent Transformer (RoMAT), which introduced role modeling to 
mitigate agent heterogeneity and improve generalization performance 
across tasks. Focusing on value estimation, Li et al. (2024) and Zhao 
et al. (2025) employed Transformer-based critics to serialize interaction 
information, achieving stable value decomposition.
Although attention mechanisms have been widely explored in MARL, 
most existing studies focus on communication design, neighbor selec-
tion, or general interaction modeling. By contrast, our objective is to 
improve the centralized critic for long-horizon cooperative trajectory 
planning, where the importance of other agents varies with relative dis-
tance, obstacle layout, and ﬂight phase. To this end, we introduce a 
self-attention-based critic to emphasize task-relevant interactions and 
suppress redundant information during value estimation. Moreover, this 
critic is integrated with dense reward shaping: the planning prior im-
proves exploration under sparse rewards, while the attention mecha-
nism enhances critic robustness and cross-agent information aggrega-
tion. Therefore, the contribution of AGP-MADDPG lies not in adopting 
attention alone, but in coupling attention-based value estimation with 
planning-guided reward shaping for long-horizon 3D cooperative multi-
UAV trajectory planning.

each UAV, and then constructs a look-ahead guidance point along the 
path at each time step to shape a dense guidance reward. A multi-head 
self-attention based critic is further adopted for interaction value esti-
mation, and temporally correlated OU noise with an annealing schedule 
is used for stable exploration. Algorithm 1 presents the pseudo code of 
the proposed AGP-MADDPG method.

the proposed framework: the planner should produce an interpretable 
and repeatable global path while allowing explicit multi-objective cost 
design. This requirement is naturally supported by A*, whose evalu-
ation function can be augmented with task-dependent penalty terms.

In our experiments, the guidance point reward acts as a soft prior 
for exploration rather than a hard trajectory-tracking constraint. Since 
the ﬁnal objective also includes task reward and safety penalties, the 
learned policy is not required to strictly follow the A* reference path 
and can depart from it when necessary for task completion or safety. 
Moreover, because the guidance point is recomputed online from the 
UAV’s real-time position, the guidance remains adaptive when the exe-
cuted trajectory departs from the reference path. In general, the dense 
guidance reward serves as a shaping term and is combined with other 
rewards to form the ﬁnal training signal. This design provides consistent 
and smooth gradients throughout the training, improving convergence

3.5.  Exploration with adaptive OU noise
OU noise is injected into the policy outputs to induce exploration that 
better conforms to the continuity constraints of UAV dynamics. Com-
pared with Gaussian noise, the OU process exhibits mean reversion and 
temporal correlation, making consecutive noise samples statistically de-
pendent. As a result, the induced action perturbations are smoother over 
time, which mitigates irregular jitter and reduces the risk of unstable 
control and safety violations.

4.  Experiments and results
4.1.  Simulation environment
To evaluate the eﬀectiveness of the proposed method, a simulation 
environment for multi-UAV autonomous obstacle avoidance and trajec-
tory planning was constructed. The simulation was conducted in a 3D 
continuous space with discrete time steps. The experimental scenarios 
were categorized into two types: basic and extended scenarios. The ba-
sic scenarios include three types of threat zones: severe-weather zones, 
no-ﬂy zones, and radar-detection zones. In the extended scenarios, the 
number of UAVs is increased from three to six, while the map scale is 
enlarged. Furthermore, threat density is intensiﬁed by expanding the 
range of threats and increasing the number of them. Each UAV in the 
simulation environment is equipped with an active threat detection sys-
tem. When a threat zone falls within the UAV’s detection range, the 
UAV acquires real-time information regarding the threats, including the 
center coordinates, radius, and type of the threat. UAVs can exchange 
position information in real time when they are within the communica-
tion range.
The experiments were conducted on a workstation with an i7-
12800HX CPU, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, and 32 
GB RAM. The software environment included Microsoft Windows 11, 
Python 3.13.5, PyTorch 2.9.1, and CUDA 13.0. Each UAV is controlled 
by an independent actor 𝜋𝑖 that maps local observations to continuous

4.2.  Evaluation of improved A* reference path generation
To justify the choice of improved A* as the reference path planner 
in our framework, we conducted comparative experiments among im-
proved A*, APF, and RRT*, which are representative path planning al-
gorithms. The experiments were performed in 50 randomly generated 
multi-UAV cooperative planning scenarios. In each scenario, both the 
obstacle locations and the start–goal conﬁgurations of the UAVs were 
randomly generated. The environment setting is illustrated in Fig. 5. 
Evaluation metrics include cooperative mean path length, minimum ob-
stacle clearance, minimum inter-UAV separation, and path smoothness. 
The mean path length is deﬁned as the average length of the planned 
paths of all UAVs. The minimum obstacle clearance denotes the mini-
mum distance from any point on the path to the surface of the nearest 
obstacle. The minimum inter-UAV separation is deﬁned as the minimum 
distance between the planned paths of any two UAVs. Path smoothness 
is used to characterize the abruptness of directional changes along the 
path, and a smaller value indicates a smoother and more regular trajec-
tory.

posed framework is compared with APF and RRT* in terms of eﬃ-
ciency, safety, and trajectory quality. APF achieves the shortest mean 
path length, 24.24 ± 4.47, indicating relatively high path eﬃciency in 
successful cases. It also yields the largest minimum inter-UAV sep-
aration, 0.71 ± 0.07, suggesting better path dispersion among agents.

4.3.  Performance evaluation in basic scenarios
Experiments were conducted in the simulation environment. We in-
vestigated the eﬀect of incorporating attention-based value estimation 
and dense guidance rewards. The compared baselines include several 
representative RL methods: Independent Deep Deterministic Policy Gra-
dient (IDDPG), MADDPG, Multi-Agent Twin Delayed Deep Determinis-
tic Policy Gradient (MATD3), and Attention Mechanism based Multi-
Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (AM-MADDPG) (Xing et al., 
2025). To provide a more comprehensive comparison with recent repre-
sentative hybrid planning-RL methods, Potential Field-Guided Improved 
Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (PG-ITD3) (Ren et al., 
2025) and Soft Actor-Critic with Artiﬁcial Potential Field guidance 
(SAC-APF) (Jin et al., 2025) were also included as baselines. PG-ITD3 
employs an improved TD3 framework to learn an adaptive policy for 
adjusting the repulsive gain coeﬃcients in the method, while SAC-APF 
integrates SAC-based global decision-making with APF-based local ob-
stacle avoidance.
For a fair comparison, all methods were trained using the same en-
vironmental settings and action bounds. During evaluation, all meth-
ods were tested under the same random seeds, number of test episodes, 
and Gaussian action noise, while algorithm-speciﬁc modules were kept 
consistent with their original designs. Fig. 6 shows the training reward 
curves of diﬀerent algorithms. All curves are averaged over 𝑆train = 3
random seeds. The solid line shows the mean, and the shaded region 
denotes the standard deviation across diﬀerent seeds.
The experimental results demonstrate that the standard MAD-
DPG requires approximately 6,000 episodes to achieve convergence. 
In contrast, AGP-MADDPG, which integrates dense guidance rewards 
and multi-head self-attention, reaches convergence after approximately 
4,000 episodes, reducing the number of training episodes to con-
vergence by 33.3%. Moreover, AGP-MADDPG achieves a substantially 
higher cumulative reward than the baselines. This suggests that the 
proposed architectural enhancements not only accelerate the learning 
process but also promote more eﬀective exploration, leading to im-
proved ﬂight trajectories. In comparative experiments, AGP-MADDPG 
consistently demonstrates superior performance. It converges roughly 
3,000 episodes earlier than IDDPG while attaining a markedly higher 
ﬁnal reward. Compared to MATD3, AGP-MADDPG exhibits faster con-
vergence and improved training stability. For SAC-APF, although SAC-
based global decision-making and APF-based local obstacle avoidance 
improve its convergence performance, its target search oriented hybrid 
design is less eﬀective for cooperative long-horizon multi-UAV trajec-
tory planning. In contrast, AGP-MADDPG further combines global ref-
erence path guidance with attention-based cooperative value estima-
tion. Relative to PG-ITD3, AGP-MADDPG achieves a higher ﬁnal reward 
and faster convergence. PG-ITD3 combines improved APF with ITD3 to 
adaptively tune repulsive gain coeﬃcients; however, its reliance on local 
potential-ﬁeld information and indirect control through repulsive-gain 
adjustment limit its performance in cooperative long-horizon multi-UAV 
planning. Although AM-MADDPG alleviates redundant interaction noise 
and input-dimension inﬂation introduced by direct concatenation in the 
conventional centralized critic, AGP-MADDPG incorporates a planning 
prior guided learning framework, thereby enhancing exploration eﬃ-
ciency. Consequently, AGP-MADDPG achieves additional gains in both 
convergence speed and performance. Overall, these results validate the 
eﬀectiveness of the proposed attention-based critic and dense guidance 
reward design for cooperative multi-agent trajectory planning. To pro-

help of the guidance reward, and its cumulative reward remains higher 
than that of the model without guidance. However, its training stability 
and ﬁnal performance are still inferior to those of the complete AGP-
MADDPG. These results indicate that planning priors contribute more 
directly to exploration eﬃciency and the performance upper bound, 
while the attention module further improves the stability of multi-agent 
value estimation and the robustness of cooperative decision-making.
The results in Table 6 further support the above observations and 
quantitatively demonstrate the complementary roles of the two key com-
ponents. In particular, removing the guidance point module leads to a 
more pronounced increase in both the average number of decision steps 
and the trajectory length, indicating that the guidance point reward 
plays a role in reducing unnecessary detours and improving path eﬃ-
ciency. This result suggests that dense path-guided feedback can provide 
more informative intermediate signals for long-horizon exploration. In 
contrast, removing the attention module results in a more noticeable 
drop in mission success rate, suggesting that the attention-based critic 
is also indispensable for suppressing redundant interaction noise and 
enhancing coordination stability among UAVs.
The contribution of each component is further evaluated through 
ablation studies in the extended scenarios, as shown in Table 7. Com-
pared with the basic scenarios, the performance gaps among diﬀerent 
variants become more pronounced as the map scale, UAV number, and 
threat density increase. Speciﬁcally, removing the guidance point mod-
ule leads to larger increases in decision steps and trajectory length, while 
removing the attention module results in a more evident decrease in 
mission success rate. These results are consistent with the observations 
in the basic scenarios and further demonstrate that the guidance point 
module mainly improves path eﬃciency, whereas the attention-based 
critic enhances coordination robustness in more complex multi-UAV en-
vironments. Overall, the ablation studies show that the guidance point 
module and the attention module jointly improve the performance of 
AGP-MADDPG in the multi-UAV trajectory planning task.
4.6.  Performance analysis under varying UAV numbers
The impact of UAV swarm size on the scalability of AGP-MADDPG is 
assessed through additional evaluations in the extended scenarios with 
4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 UAVs. This experiment analyzes how the swarm size 
aﬀects cooperative decision eﬃciency, trajectory quality, and mission 
reliability, while keeping the evaluation conditions consistent with the 
previous experiments for comparability. As illustrated in Fig. 10, the 
trajectories become increasingly dense as the UAV swarm size grows, 
indicating stronger inter-agent coupling and more frequent local coor-
dination requirements. Table 8 further quantiﬁes these observations by 
reporting the corresponding performance metrics under diﬀerent UAV 
numbers.
The quantitative results are consistent with the trajectory visualiza-
tion. As shown in Table 8, the overall performance of AGP-MADDPG 
gradually decreases as the UAV swarm size increases. When the number 
of UAVs increases from 4 to 12, the mission success rate decreases from 
93.7% to 70.3%, while the average decision steps and mean trajectory 
length increase from 28.10 to 45.28 and from 13.86 to 21.86. These re-
sults indicate that larger UAV swarms introduce stronger inter-agent 
coupling, more frequent collision-avoidance adjustments, and longer 
detoured trajectories in the evaluated 3D environment. Nevertheless,

5.  Conclusions
Cooperative multi-UAV trajectory planning involves sparse rewards, 
long-horizon credit assignment, and multi-agent coordination. To ad-
dress these issues, we proposed a hybrid MARL framework named AGP-
MADDPG. We developed an improved A* planner with multi-objective 
constraints to generate global reference paths that optimize path length, 
safety margin, and inter-UAV separation. Based on real-time UAV states, 
a guidance point mechanism was introduced to convert sparse termi-
nal feedback into dense guidance rewards, alleviating ineﬃcient ex-
ploration and slow convergence in long-horizon tasks. Under the CTDE 
paradigm, we incorporated a multi-head self-attention centralized critic 
to adaptively aggregate and weight cross-agent interaction information, 
improving critic robustness. In addition, an adaptive OU noise strategy 
was employed to support smooth and stable exploration consistent with 
continuous UAV control dynamics. Simulation results show that AGP-
MADDPG consistently outperforms baselines in both basic and extended 
scenarios, achieving better performance in convergence speed, mission 
success rate, decision eﬃciency, and trajectory quality. Nevertheless, 
the current study was conducted in simulated environments with mod-
erate obstacle densities and static threat conﬁgurations. Further valida-
tion in more cluttered and dynamic environments is necessary to assess 
the broader applicability of the proposed framework. In addition, the 
observed performance degradation as the number of UAVs increases in-
dicates that further improvements are required to support coordination 
in larger-scale multi-UAV systems. Therefore, future work will evaluate 
the framework in more demanding environments with higher obstacle 
densities and dynamic uncertainties. In addition, given that the present 
evaluation is simulation-based, AGP-MADDPG will be further validated 
on physical quadrotor platforms in the presence of real-world sensing 
noise, communication delays, and environmental disturbances.
